## How values are obtained

- **Positions, object type, morphology string, magnitudes, radial velocity**: SIMBAD (CfA mirror TAP service), tables `basic`, `flux`, `mesDistance`, `ident`. The morphology string is the catalogue's own; `galaxyClass` is derived from it by `classifyGalaxy` (`src/core/deepspace/classify.ts`). When SIMBAD's string is numeric-only or cannot be classified, HyperLEDA's `type` is used and the other string is recorded in the object's facts.
- **Galaxy angular size (D25), axis ratio, position angle, inclination**: HyperLEDA `ledacat.cgi`. D25 = 10^logd25 × 0.1 arcmin. HyperLEDA publishes no uncertainty for these. Its inclination is, per the documented HyperLEDA procedure (not verified in this build), computed from the axis ratio and type; it is **not** used for ellipticals or for the Sombrero (whose HyperLEDA axis ratio gives an inclination far from the edge-on appearance).
- **Distances**: always redshift-independent for the named objects. One primary measurement per object, chosen as described below; the values are read from the paper's abstract (checked against the fetched text), from a NED-D record, or from a VizieR catalogue row. The other values SIMBAD lists, and the spread of NED-D records, are given in each object's facts so that the single drawn value is not mistaken for the only one.
- **Derived values** (physical diameter = 2 d tan(θ/2), absolute magnitude, age in Myr, distances from a distance modulus) are `kind: "derived"` with their `transformations`. Uncertainty is propagated from the distance only (no angular-size or photometric-calibration uncertainty is published for those inputs). Absolute magnitudes are **not** corrected for extinction.
- **Combined uncertainties**: where a paper gives statistical and systematic terms, the stored uncertainty is their quadrature sum and the note says so; asymmetric intervals are stored as their mean half-width and the note gives the asymmetric values.

## What each value is

| measurement key | meaning |
|---|---|
| `distance` | primary distance (same object as `sky.distance`) |
| `distanceModulus` | (m-M) as tabulated by the source, when the distance was computed from it |
| `magB`, `magV`, `magK` | SIMBAD apparent integrated magnitudes (bibcode in the source) |
| `magB_total_hyperleda`, `magV_total_harris` | catalogue total magnitudes |
| `absMagB`, `absMagV` | derived, **no extinction correction** |
| `physicalDiameterD25` / `physicalDiameter` | derived projected linear diameter from the stated angular size definition |
| `radialVelocity` | heliocentric, SIMBAD (type flag in the note) |
| `memberCount` | open clusters: Hunt & Reffert 2023 member stars; groups: rows passing the stated rule |
| `memberCountProperMotion`/`...RadialVelocity` | globular clusters: stars with measurements used in Baumgardt's fits (not a census) |
| `logAge`, `age` | Hunt & Reffert 2023 model estimate (open clusters); `age` is the derived Myr conversion |
| `mass` | globular clusters: Baumgardt N-body fit mass (modelled) |
| `metallicityFeH` | [Fe/H] from Dias et al. 2021 (open) or the Harris catalogue (globular) |
| `halfNumberRadius`, `tidalRadius`, `totalRadius` | Hunt & Reffert radii converted to pc |

## Choices that are ours (not published)

- The reflection nebula is **ngc-1999** (the roster allowed any of M78, NGC 1999, NGC 7023, Pleiades nebulosity). The build uses the first candidate unless the nebula image manifest names a different reflection-nebula object id that the roster knows.
- The Zucker et al. (2020) distance of a nebula is **modelled** (a 3D dust-extinction model along sightlines): the median of d50 over ALL catalogued sightlines within 1° of the object's SIMBAD position; the stored uncertainty is sqrt((half the full range of those medians)² + (median catalogue error)²), a descriptive scatter by our rule. All sightlines are listed in the measurement's transformations. It is the distance of the dust cloud, not of the nebula or its illuminating stars.
- Virgo Cluster members (203 galaxies) are the 2MRS rows within 6° of M87 with −500 ≤ cz ≤ 2500 km/s. This window is ours, not a published membership: it includes the blueshifted infalling members (M86, M90, M98) but is still contaminated by foreground and background galaxies.
- M81 Group members (36 galaxies) are M81 plus the UNGC rows with main disturber M81 and tidal index Θ1 > 0, which is the group definition printed in the UNGC paper (verified against the paper text by the build).
- Local Group members are all rows of the McConnachie (2012) file, including the "nearby neighbour" rows; the subgroup flag stays in the point file.
- Disc orientation: `nearSide` is `unknown` for every galaxy: none of the sources retrieved here states which side is nearer. M31, M33 and M81 use HyperLEDA inclination/PA; the LMC uses the classical-Cepheid fit of Inno et al. 2016 (an old-population tracer would give different values, as that paper reports).
- Exaggerations: the data apply no size boost to any object. Listed under `representation.exaggerations`: deprojection (stretching by 1/cos i), the flattening of Virgo depth to a cluster-mean distance, the hash-seeded illustrative line-of-sight offsets of globular-cluster stars (depth `illustrative`), the brightness-split illustrative depth layers of the nebulae (depth `illustrative-layers`) and the smooth drawing of Milky Way arms. A renderer that enlarges markers must declare that itself.

## Milky Way model

`models["milky-way"]`: 48 parameters, 2 tables. Every parameter value was matched to a string in the fetched source text during the build (Bland-Hawthorn & Gerhard 2016 through ar5iv, Reid et al. 2019 through ar5iv, GRAVITY 2019 and Reid 2019 abstracts). Table `spiral-arms-reid2019` is Reid et al. (2019) Table 2 parsed from the ar5iv page. Limitations are listed in the model and shown below.

- Spiral arms are log-periodic fits to ~200 maser parallaxes of high-mass star-forming regions (Reid et al. 2019); they describe the young, gas-rich tracers only, and the old stellar disc is not spiral in this model.
- Arm centre lines are only constrained over the azimuth range betaMin..betaMax in the table; everywhere else (including most of the far side of the Galaxy, behind the Galactic Centre) the drawn arms are extrapolations of the fitted spiral, not observations.
- In the fourth Galactic quadrant no maser parallaxes existed for the 2019 fits; arm positions there are constrained by tangency-longitude priors from CO and H I emission (Reid et al. 2019, Table 2 notes).
- Dust extinction hides much of the disc and bulge from optical surveys; the far-side and obscured structure is inferred from infrared star counts, kinematics and models (Bland-Hawthorn & Gerhard 2016), and several parameters differ between methods.
- Disc scale lengths and heights are described by the review itself as uncertain ("we are not able to present a fully consistent picture for the disk at this time"); the values here are the review's recommended means with its stated errors, not a fit to one data set.
- Different parameters come from sources that assume different Sun-Galactic Centre distances (8.15 kpc Reid 2019, 8.2 kpc Bland-Hawthorn & Gerhard 2016, 8.178 kpc GRAVITY 2019); they are listed separately and not rescaled to a common value.
- No warp, flare, or individual-star positions are included: drawing them would require data this model does not contain.

The VizieR table J/ApJ/885/131 holds the maser parallaxes (Reid et al. 2019 Table 1), not the arm fits; the arm table therefore comes from the paper text.
